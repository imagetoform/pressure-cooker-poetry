# BRAIN CANDY: the arcade plan

Decided in CANON D-30. Built so far: the hub (`/brain-candy/`) and Machine 01, The Spin Cycle (`/brain-candy/spin-cycle/`).

## The one rule

Every machine does a trick **to** you, then opens itself up. **Play → Reveal → Learn.**

- **Play:** you work the trick yourself, in a toy with a goal, a score and a clock.
- **Reveal:** an X-ray that shows each move and what it hides.
- **Learn:** the labels: what it's called, one question to ask, where the idea comes from, with sources.

## House rules

- No live AI calls (CANON D-26). Machines run on hand-written content, templates and simple maths, so they work offline and never invent facts.
- Fiction is labelled. Real techniques, invented companies. Anything sourced is kept apart from anything satirical.
- Satire points up: bosses, landlords, empires, ad men. Never at the people underneath (D-12).
- No accounts, no tracking. The wrapper jar and settings live in the visitor's own browser.
- Touch, mouse and keyboard all work. Reduced motion is respected. Sound is synthesized live and can be muted.
- The score is never the point. Every end screen says what you can now spot in the real world, "including when it's us doing it."

## Progression: the wrapper jar → Department Zero

- Every technique you **catch** (not just read about) drops a candy wrapper into your jar.
- A full jar opens a **Department Zero** file: a short cipher puzzle. Each file teaches a real historical code and decodes to one sentence of the site's philosophy.
  - File 001 (built): ROT13 / Caesar shift → "NOTHING IS INEVITABLE"
  - File 002: Morse, hidden in a machine's sound effects
  - File 003: Pigpen cipher, scratched into a cutout
  - File 004: a book cipher keyed to one of Brandon's poems (the poem is only read, never changed)
  - File 005: a message hidden in an image's pixels (steganography)
- Escape-room rules from the brief: clues work with touch and keyboard, need no developer tools, never block normal navigation, and always have a way out.

## The machines

| # | Machine | Play | Reveal | Learn (backbone) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | **The Spin Cycle** (built) | You're the PR flack. Load 3 of 8 detergents into a washer, spin a dirty fact into a press release. Then switch sides: catch someone else's spin on a timer, combo scoring. | X-ray: each sentence's trick, what it hides, the question to ask, and what actually happened. | IPA's seven devices (1937) + fog (Orwell 1946, Lutz, Carlin). |
| 02 | **Bias Factory** | A conveyor belt of quick guesses. Before each one, the belt flashes a "random" number, a framing, or a hand-picked sample. | A graph of your own guesses drifting toward whatever the belt showed you first. | Anchoring and framing (Tversky & Kahneman), survivorship bias (Abraham Wald's bomber-armour problem). |
| 03 | **Red-String Machine** | Pin any 4 random facts to a corkboard. The machine strings them into a theory that sounds airtight. You try to make the most convincing one. | Every move tagged: "just asking questions", coincidence-as-pattern, unfalsifiable claim, moving the goalposts. | Apophenia; connection vs evidence. A separate, sourced shelf for conspiracies that were real and documented, clearly apart from the fiction. |
| 04 | **S.O.F.T.** (euphemism escalator) | Ride a hard word up the elevator: each floor makes it longer, vaguer, more Latin, less painful. Then go down: take corporate mush back to plain words; score by syllables cut. | Floor by floor, which softening rule did what. | Carlin's shell-shock chain; doublespeak. Link to the Tongue & Dagger version instead of forking it. |
| 05 | **Hall of Mirrors** (new idea) | One event, four outlets. Slide between frames and watch the same facts tilt: who's the subject, which verb, which photo, what comes first. | The sentence diff: what changed between mirrors. | Framing and agenda-setting; active vs passive headlines. |
| 06 | **Ransom Note Studio** (new idea; a "make" machine) | After all the taking-apart, put something together: cut your own counter-slogan out of the site's cutout letters, poster-style. | Your note as a tiny zine page you can save. | Zines, samizdat, broadsides: how people without printing presses published anyway. Ties to the cutout library and to "give people their voices back." |
