Brain Candy

Take language apart.

Speeches, contracts, ads, poems: somebody built every one of those sentences to do something to you. These are tools for taking them apart and building something of your own out of the pieces. Every method here was invented by a writer, and every one comes with who, when, and where to read more.

Open the workbench → A marker, a correction brush, a hobby knife, glue, and a stack of pages: the Declaration, Eisenhower’s warning about the military-industrial complex, a terms-of-service, every poem on this site, or your own words. “Cut ups are for everyone.” W. S. Burroughs

The methods

Erase · newspaper tradition

Blackout

A fat marker. Cross out everything you don’t want. What’s left standing is the poem.

Grab the marker Erase · the whiteout poets

Erasure

A correction brush. Paint words out to nothing, so the page goes quiet instead of black.

Grab the brush Cut · Brion Gysin & William S. Burroughs, 1959

The Cut-Up

A blade through the page, straight through the middle of words. Drag the scraps around, glue down the ones that mean something now.

Grab the knife Cut · Burroughs’ own recipe

Burroughs ×4

Cut the page down the middle and across the middle. Put section four with one, two with three. Read the new page.

Quarter a page Fold · Gysin & Burroughs, early 1960s

The Fold-In

Fold two typed pages in half and lay them together, so every line starts in one text and finishes in the other.

Fold two pages Constraint · Jean Lescure, Oulipo, 1961

N+7

Swap every noun for the seventh noun after it in the dictionary. Watch a founding document turn strange.

Run N+7 Chance · John Cage

Mesostic

A word runs down the middle of the poem, and the lines grow out of a source text letter by letter.

Spell one Machine · Brion Gysin & Ian Sommerville

Permutations

One short phrase, printed in every order its words can go. Some of the earliest computer-made poems.

Run the machine Voice · Hugo Ball, Cabaret Voltaire, 1916

Sound Poem

Verses without words, shaken loose from a real text and set in clashing type. Your device can perform it out loud.

Make noise

Nothing here needs an account, and nothing you make leaves your browser unless you copy it out. Every poem on this site can go on the bench too: hit Cut this up at the bottom of any poem.

Coming to the bench

  • Tzara’s hat. Words in a hat, drawn one at a time.
  • The broken typewriter. Perec wrote a whole novel without the letter E.
  • Kerouac’s scroll. One long roll, no backspace.
  • 100,000 billion poems. Queneau’s sonnets cut into strips you flip.
  • Cage’s coins. Chance decides which words survive.
  • The exquisite corpse. Fold the paper, pass it on.
  • The cento. A poem built only from other poets’ lines.
  • Automatic writing. Write until the timer goes off.
  • The treated page. Paint over a page and leave rivers of words.
  • Tape cut-ups. Record yourself, splice the tape.
